Sensor-integrated wear for strength training.

Movement feedback while you train.

Demson is developing compression wear that uses fabric stretch sensors to track strength movements and provide audio form cues.

How it works ↓
Concept in development · Montréal
0104

Feedback during the set

Form can change
as fatigue builds.

As a set progresses, changes in depth, balance, or bracing can be difficult to notice. A coach may not always be present, and cameras require space and a clear view.

Demson is intended for people who train independently and want useful movement feedback before the set is over.

How it is designed to work

From fabric stretch
to movement data.

Fabric sensors measure how the garment stretches. Software then interprets those signals as changes in movement during a rep.

1

Measure stretch.

Fabric sensors respond as the garment changes shape during movement.

2

Interpret movement.

Software is designed to map the sensor signals to each phase of a rep.

3

Provide a cue.

The planned coaching system will send brief form cues through the athlete’s earbuds.

Sensors within the garment

Designed to train in.
No camera required.

Demson’s approach places fabric stretch sensors within compression training wear. The aim is to collect movement data without setting up a camera or attaching rigid sensors.

Built into the material

Sensors made
for training wear.

The proposed system uses a technical knit with conductive stretch sensors. The design goal is a breathable garment that fits and moves like familiar compression wear.
